Write in English or Spanish, and tell me the name the invitation should be issued to. I'll reply with a link. Open it on the iPhone or iPad you intend to use, and the welcome screen fills itself in.

Why it works this way

Callbook has no accounts and no server. Everything you write is encrypted on your own device with a passphrase only you know — which means that if you forget it, nobody, including me, can get your notes back. That is the point of the design, and it is also why handing the app to strangers is a bad idea: there is nobody to call when it goes wrong except the person who wrote it.

So it goes out one person at a time. An invitation is a link signed with a key I hold; the app checks the signature on the device, without contacting anything. Nothing about you is sent anywhere when you use it, and there is nothing for me to look up afterwards.

What it does

  • People you call on, when you last went, and when you said you would go back.
  • Bible studies, with their own history.
  • Service hours, split into ministry and credit, and the monthly report they add up to.
  • Hour goals for regular, auxiliary and special pioneers. Publishers can log hours too — their report simply doesn't have a line for them.
  • English and Spanish. Your notes, a map of your calls, and an encrypted backup you keep yourself.

It needs iOS 18 or later. It was called Agenda before; same app, clearer name.

Open the link within two weeks

A link has to be opened within fourteen days of being sent. That is not about you — it is because a link that stays good forever ends up forwarded into a group chat, and there is nothing on your phone that could notice. After two weeks it simply stops working, so a stray copy is worth nothing to whoever finds it.

Once you have opened it, that's it — your app never expires. Reinstalling later, changing phones, or restoring your own backup will ask for a link again only if your device has forgotten this one; write to me and I'll send a fresh link the same day.

The link is not a password and it is not a key. It opens the door once, at first run; your passphrase is what encrypts your data, and the two have nothing to do with each other.
