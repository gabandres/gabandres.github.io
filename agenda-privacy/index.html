Agenda for iOS

Privacy Policy

Agenda keeps a private record of return visits and bible studies. Everything you write is encrypted on your iPhone with a password only you know. There are no analytics, and nothing readable ever leaves your device.

What Agenda stores

Everything you enter — names, addresses, phone numbers, notes, visit dates, study details and service hours — is written to an encrypted database file inside the app's own storage on your device.

That database is encrypted with SQLCipher. The encryption key is random, and is itself protected by the password you choose when you create your account: your password derives a key (via scrypt) that unwraps the database key using AES-256-GCM.

Your password is never sent anywhere and nothing checks it. There is no login server to check it against. It is not a credential that is verified — it is what produces the key, and a wrong password simply produces bytes that do not decrypt. That is also why the same password works on a second device: it is the only thing the two devices share, and it is what lets you sign in somewhere new and get your callbook back.

The wrapped key is stored in two places. One copy is in the iOS Keychain, marked so that it never leaves this device. A second copy is written to a file in the app's own storage, which means it is included in an iCloud Backup of your iPhone — deliberately, because otherwise restoring a new phone from a backup would give you the encrypted database and nothing to open it with. Both copies are wrapped: they are useless without your password.

Nobody can recover your password, including the developer. If you forget it, the data is permanently unreadable — on your device and in the iCloud copy, because the same key seals both. This is a deliberate design choice, not an oversight: an app with no server cannot reset what it has no way to verify.

What Agenda never does

  • It never sends your callbook — the people you visit, your notes, your hours — anywhere anyone else can read. Not the developer, not Apple. The one exception is the name and email address on your account, described below.
  • It has no login server. Your password is never transmitted and never checked by anyone — it only ever derives the key on your own device.
  • It never stores your password, or a hash of it, anywhere. There is nothing to leak and nothing to reset.
  • It contains no analytics, telemetry, advertising or tracking of any kind.
  • It contains no third-party advertising or marketing SDKs.
  • It does not share, sell or disclose your information to anyone.

Your account

Callbook is by approval, so it needs to know who you are. When you create an account you give a name and an email address, and choose a password. You can supply the name and address in two ways:

  • Sign in with Apple — Apple confirms them, so the person approving access knows the address is real. If you choose Hide My Email, Apple gives a private forwarding address instead and Callbook never sees your real one. Apple also provides an identifier for this app alone, which identifies you nowhere else.
  • Type them yourself — if you would rather not use Sign in with Apple, or it is unavailable. Nothing is verified in that case, and the person approving access is told so.

The password is not part of your identity and is never sent with it. See above: it stays on your device and produces your encryption key.

You can sign out at any time, from Settings. That removes Callbook from the device and leaves the encrypted copy in your own iCloud untouched, so signing back in with the same email and password brings your callbook back. Erasing everything, also in Settings, destroys both.

This is the only personal information Callbook collects. Your name, your email address and the per-app identifier are sent to the developer so that access can be granted. The developer can read them, because reading them is how the decision gets made. Nobody else can — not other users of the app, who cannot see that requests exist at all. It is stored in Apple's iCloud infrastructure, in a database belonging to this app, and is not shared with anyone or used for anything except deciding whether to grant you access.

The decision itself is one record holding the anonymous identifier and whether access was granted. Your name and contact detail are not copied into it. Ask for your request to be deleted at any time using the contact address at the bottom of this page, and it will be.

Access can be withdrawn. If it is, the app stops opening on your devices — your records are not deleted and stay on your device, encrypted as they always were.

iCloud sync

Agenda can keep two of your own devices in step, and let a new iPhone pick up your agenda without restoring from a backup. Sync is off until you turn it on.

When it is on, Agenda stores a single encrypted file in your private iCloud database, using Apple's CloudKit. Private means exactly that: it lives in your own iCloud account, and the developer has no way to reach it — not through the app, and not through any dashboard Apple provides.

Before it is uploaded, that file is sealed with a key derived from your password, which never leaves your device. So it is unreadable by Apple as well. What is stored in iCloud is a block of ciphertext with no names, no addresses and no notes visible in it.

Because the key comes from your password, each device asks for it once when you enable sync. And the same warning applies with more force than before: if you forget your password, the copy in iCloud is as permanently unreadable as the one on your phone.

Turning sync off, in Agenda's settings, stops any further uploads and leaves the copy in iCloud alone. Deleting that copy is a separate action on the same screen.

Location

If you choose to save a location for a person, Agenda asks for permission to read your device's current coordinates once, at that moment. The coordinates are stored in the same encrypted database and used only to place a pin on the map inside the app.

Location is never transmitted to the developer or to anyone else, and is never visible to Apple: if sync is on, coordinates are inside the same encrypted file as everything else. The permission is optional — declining it only means you cannot capture coordinates automatically. Agenda does not track your location in the background.

Face ID

If you enable Face ID, the check is performed entirely by iOS. Agenda receives only a yes-or-no answer and never has access to your biometric data, which never leaves Apple's Secure Enclave.

Over-the-air updates

Agenda uses Expo's update service so improvements can be delivered without a full App Store release. On launch it contacts u.expo.dev to ask whether a newer version of the app's code exists.

That request carries technical information only — the platform, the app's runtime and update identifiers — and, as with any internet request, your device's IP address is visible to the server. No part of your agenda is included, and none of your entries, notes, contacts or locations are sent.

Together with iCloud sync above, this is the complete list of connections Agenda makes. There are no others.

Expo's own privacy practices are described at expo.dev/privacy.

Exporting your data

Agenda can export a copy of everything as a JSON file through the iOS share sheet. This happens only when you ask for it, and you choose where the file goes.

The exported file is not encrypted, so that you can open it on a computer. Once it leaves the app it is an ordinary file on your device, and this policy no longer governs it — handle it accordingly.

Deleting your data

"Erase everything" in Settings deletes the encrypted database and the stored key, leaving nothing recoverable. Deleting the app from your iPhone has the same effect on that device.

If you used iCloud sync, Agenda also deletes the encrypted copy in your own iCloud account. That needs a connection, so if your iPhone cannot reach iCloud at that moment, Agenda tells you so and the copy stays until you try again — though it is already unreadable by then, because the key that opened it was destroyed with everything else.

No copy exists anywhere else. There is no server of the developer's holding your data, and so nothing to request the deletion of.

Children

Agenda is a personal record-keeping tool intended for adults. It is not directed at children and does not knowingly gather information about them.
